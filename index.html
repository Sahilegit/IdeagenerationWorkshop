Scenario Summary

Polly Storm is the Director of Learning and Development for a national brewing company based in Calgary. Polly and a team of five trainers deliver mandatory classroom training to business teams across Canada.

Although business leaders support mandatory training, sessions are frequently cancelled because of last-minute scheduling changes. These cancellations create unnecessary expenses and have caused the organization to fall behind on its training objectives.

Polly and the training team would like to explore more effective ways to market and deliver mandatory training. The workshop will help the group generate ideas, prioritize the most promising options, and develop an initial action plan.

Event Details

Duration

Four hours

1:00 p.m. to 5:00 p.m.

Format

Virtual delivery using Microsoft Teams or Zoom

Participants

Polly Storm and five Learning and Development trainers

Guest

Operations Manager will attend the 30-minute operational-overview segment.

Facilitator

An external process facilitator with experience in Learning and Development, idea generation, and change management

Key Assumptions

  • The trainers understand adult-learning principles and currently deliver classroom training across Canada.
  • The trainers may hesitate to change the classroom model because they value in-person facilitation and work-related travel.
  • Some trainers may be concerned that increased use of virtual or asynchronous learning could affect their roles.
  • The facilitator is a process expert but is not the organization’s operational subject-matter expert.
  • The workshop will focus on improving the training system rather than assigning blame for cancelled sessions.
  • Ideas requiring significant investment or organizational approval will require further review after the workshop.

Pre-Work

One week before the workshop, participants will receive the agenda, workshop purpose, central brainstorming question, participation expectations, and instructions for accessing the collaboration tools.

Participants will complete a short survey addressing:

  • Factors contributing to training cancellations
  • Barriers to attendance or completion
  • Strengths of the current classroom approach
  • Ideas for improving marketing and delivery
  • Organizational constraints that should influence the final decision
The facilitator will organize the anonymous responses into themes before the workshop.

Session Objectives

By the end of the workshop, participants will be able to:

Identify and categorize

At least five barriers affecting the scheduling, promotion, attendance, or delivery of mandatory training.

Generate

At least ten feasible ideas for reformatting and marketing mandatory training so it better aligns with operational needs.

Evaluate and plan

Evaluate the ideas using agreed criteria, identify three priorities, and develop an initial action plan for the preferred approach, including next steps, ownership, required support, a target date, and measures of success.

Facilitator in Action

Facilitated Practice Exercise

Stop, Start, Continue

Duration

15 minutes

Objective

Participants will identify one behaviour to start, stop, and continue when supporting a team through workplace change.

Process

Introduction and instructions - 3 minutes

Introduce the facilitation team, state the objective, explain the Miro or Microsoft Whiteboard, and review the three prompts.

Individual reflection - 5 minutes

Participants post one response under Start, Stop, and Continue.

Roundtable and debrief - 7 minutes

Participants share one response. Facilitators ask: What patterns do you notice? Which behaviour could make the greatest difference? What would help you follow through?

Tools

Miro or Microsoft Whiteboard, Zoom chat, and a visible timer.

Plan B

If the whiteboard is unavailable, participants post responses in chat under the three headings.

Outcome

Participants leave with one practical commitment and a clearer understanding of behaviours that support change.
